# YouAnalyser 0.9.3

* Started a Segmentation (cluster analysis) module:
  * `seg_prep_data()` prepares survey data for cluster analysis: selects the clustering variables, handles missing values (listwise deletion, mean/median imputation, single `mice` imputation, or keeping them for Gower distance), optionally removes respondents above a missing-value threshold, rescales the variables (z-standardization or min-max), and computes a distance matrix.
  * `seg_distance()` computes distance matrices with a common interface to `factoextra::get_dist()` and `cluster::daisy()` (incl. Gower distance).
  * `seg_clustering_tendency()` assesses whether the data contains meaningful clusters by comparing the actual data with a uniformly distributed random dataset, using the (corrected) Hopkins statistic and VAT plots (`factoextra::get_clust_tendency()`, `factoextra::fviz_dist()`).
  * `seg_optimal_clusters()` determines the optimal number of clusters with the elbow method (WSS), the average silhouette method, the gap statistic and the majority vote of 26 NbClust indices, and summarizes their suggestions. The individual methods are also available as `seg_nbclust_wss()`, `seg_nbclust_silhouette()`, `seg_nbclust_gap()` and `seg_nbclust_indices()`.
  * `seg_compare_algorithms()` compares clustering algorithms (hierarchical, k-means, PAM, DIANA, CLARA) and numbers of clusters with the internal and stability validation measures of the `clValid` package, reports the size of the smallest cluster of each solution, and plots all measures with ggplot2.
  * `seg_cluster()` runs a cluster analysis with hierarchical k-means (default), k-means, PAM, AGNES, DIANA, fuzzy clustering, model-based clustering, DBSCAN or HDBSCAN, for one or several numbers of clusters. All methods return a standardized solution (cluster assignment renumbered by size, membership degrees/probabilities, cluster sizes and centers, the prepared and original data) with a cluster plot in the space of the principal components, a dendrogram (hierarchical methods) and a heatmap. The method families are also available as `seg_cluster_partitioning()`, `seg_cluster_hkmeans()`, `seg_cluster_hierarchical()`, `seg_cluster_fuzzy()`, `seg_cluster_model()` and `seg_cluster_density()`.
  * `seg_dbscan_eps()` suggests `eps` for DBSCAN from the k-nearest-neighbor distances.
  * `seg_as_cluster()` converts an existing segmentation (e.g. from a previous wave) into the same format, so that it can be visualized, profiled and validated like new solutions.
  * `seg_plot_cluster()`, `seg_plot_dend()` and `seg_plot_heatmap()` (re)create the plots of a solution, passing further arguments to `factoextra::fviz_cluster()` and `factoextra::fviz_dend()`. The heatmap is built with ggplot2 for a random sample of respondents, with dendrograms of the variables and the respondents and a cluster color bar; respondents can be grouped by cluster or ordered by a hierarchical clustering.
  * `seg_plot_centers()` plots the profiles of the clusters, i.e. the mean of each clustering variable per cluster, as profile lines (default), bars per cluster or a heatmap, on the prepared (e.g. z-standardized) or the original scale, with the total mean as reference, the variables labelled by their variable labels and optionally sorted by how much the clusters differ. Every solution of `seg_cluster()`, its family functions and `seg_as_cluster()` now includes it as `plots$centers`, customizable with the new argument `centers_args`.
  * `seg_validation()` validates one or several cluster solutions (several numbers of clusters and/or algorithms) with internal measures (average silhouette width, share of negative silhouette widths, Dunn index and further statistics of `fpc::cluster.stats()`) and, if a reference grouping is supplied (a vector, a column of the original data or an existing segmentation), external measures (corrected Rand index, Meila's variation of information) with cross tabulations. It returns silhouette plots (`factoextra::fviz_silhouette()`) and ggplot2 plots of all measures over the number of clusters, and reports the best solution per measure.
  * `seg_plot_silhouette()` (re)creates the silhouette plot of a solution.
  * `seg_agreement()` compares the assignments of several cluster solutions computed on the same respondents, e.g. different algorithms with the same number of clusters. The clusters of each solution are matched to those of a reference solution with the Hungarian algorithm (`clue::solve_LSAP()`), so that cluster numbers are comparable across solutions. It reports the pairwise agreement (share of respondents in matched clusters), corrected Rand index and variation of information, a heatmap of the pairwise agreement, cross tables with the reference, and the matched cluster of each respondent in each solution, together with how many solutions agree on it. The solutions are also returned with their clusters renumbered to match the reference (sizes, centers, assignment, memberships and plots), so that their cluster profiles can be compared directly. `clue` is suggested.
  * `predict()` assigns new respondents (e.g. a new wave) to the clusters of a `seg_cluster` solution. The new data is prepared exactly like the original sample: missing values are handled with the same strategy (`mice` imputes them with a model estimated on the original sample only), and the variables are rescaled with the parameters of the original sample. Each method uses its own assignment rule: nearest center (k-means, hierarchical k-means), nearest medoid (PAM), the linkage of the solution (AGNES, DIANA), membership degrees from the `fanny` objective (fuzzy), posterior probabilities (`mclust`), and the nearest clustered respondent within `eps` or the core distance (DBSCAN, HDBSCAN). Alternatives are `rule = "centroid"` and `rule = "knn"` (k nearest neighbors). `type = "prob"` returns membership probabilities. Distances to the original sample are available for all distance measures of `seg_prep_data()`, with the Gower ranges of the original sample.
  * `seg_prep_data()` now stores the rescaling parameters (`settings$scaling`), the imputation values of mean / median imputation and the `mice` arguments. `seg_cluster()` solutions keep them (`prep`), together with the renumbering of the clusters (`cluster_map`).
  * Added a segmentation template script (`inst/templates/seg.R`), available with `ya_setup_folder_structure(template = "seg")`. It follows the segmentation vignette in short with the recommended settings (mice imputation, hierarchical k-means): data preparation, clustering tendency, number of clusters, validation and robustness across algorithms, interpretation with cluster profiles and `eda_` functions, export of the data with a labelled segment variable, and assignment of new respondents.
* The README now installs the package with `remotes::install_github(..., dependencies = TRUE, build_vignettes = TRUE)`, so that the vignettes are available offline (`pak` does not build vignettes), and gives an overview of the PSM and segmentation modules.
* Updated the package description in `DESCRIPTION`: it now lists the PSM and segmentation modules.
* Fixed warnings of `R CMD check`:
  * `scales` and `survey`, which were already used, are now declared in Imports. `usethis` and `rstudioapi` (used by `ya_setup_folder_structure(make_rproj = TRUE)`) are now suggested; `ya_setup_folder_structure()` asks to install them if they are missing.
  * Non-ASCII characters in the R code (e.g. "R²" in the KDA plots and messages) are now written as Unicode escapes. The output is unchanged.
  * Removed the documentation of the argument `dfSummary_args` from `eda_summary()`, which does not have this argument.
* `ya_setup_folder_structure()` no longer creates an R project by default (`make_rproj = FALSE`), checks `template` against the available templates before creating any folder, and supports the new `"seg"` template.
* The `eda_` functions no longer trigger tidyselect's deprecation warning when `vars` is given as `all_of(...)`.
* Set up unit tests with testthat (edition 3), starting with the assignment of new respondents (`tests/testthat/test-seg_predict.R`).
* `fpc` is now imported (for `fpc::cluster.stats()`).
* `tidyr`, which was already used, is now declared in Imports.
* YouAnalyser now requires R >= 4.1.0, as the package code uses the native pipe `|>` and the function shorthand `\(x)`.
* `mclust` and `dbscan` are suggested for model-based and density-based clustering.
* Added `yougov_palette`, an unnamed vector of YouGov colors for plots with groups (e.g. clusters), to be used e.g. instead of the `"jco"` palette in factoextra.
* `factoextra`, `cluster`, `NbClust` and `clValid` are now imported. `ggplot2`, which was already used, is now declared in Imports.
* Added the vignette "5. Segmentation (Cluster Analysis)", which walks through a complete segmentation (data preparation, clustering tendency, number of clusters, hierarchical k-means, internal and external validation, interpretation with `eda_explore()`) on `sm_synthetic` and `sim_segments`, and explains all metrics with common guidelines and their sources.
* Added `sim_segments`, simulated survey data with three well separated segments on 20 rating items (and the true segment membership), to illustrate the segmentation functions on data with a clear cluster structure.
* Added interactive and scriptable descriptive statistics for several variables at once (`R/eda_interactive.R`):
  * `eda_explore()` opens a Shiny gadget (viewer pane, browser or dialog) to select a dataset of the session, variables (searchable by name and variable label, or by a pattern such as a whole item battery), a grouping variable and an optional weight, and returns plots, descriptive statistics, tests of group differences, a detailed, customizable `ggstatsplot` plot of a single variable (in the YouGov colors), a codebook and the code to reproduce them. Plots are downloaded as JPEG with `ya_save_plot()`. If the output of `seg_cluster()` is supplied, each cluster solution is available as grouping variable (e.g. `cluster_k4`).
  * `eda_describe()` computes (optionally weighted) descriptive statistics by group: means, standard deviations, standard errors, confidence intervals (using Kish's effective sample size if weighted) and quantiles for numeric variables, frequency distributions for categorical variables. Variables are treated as numeric if they have at least five value labels or distinct values. User-defined missing values are set to `NA` first.
  * `eda_test()` tests group differences of numeric variables with (weighted) ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis tests (effect sizes from `effectsize`) and compares the groups with linear contrasts of a linear model with robust (HC3) standard errors via `modelbased::estimate_contrasts()` (on the ranks if nonparametric, Holm-adjusted): each group vs. the total including the group (default), vs. the rest of the sample, vs. the mean of the group means, or all pairs of groups. Categorical variables are tested with chi-square tests (Cramer's V). The tests are explained in detail in `?eda_test`.
  * `eda_plot_detail()` plots a single variable by group with the results of statistical tests (`ggstatsplot::ggbetweenstats()` / `ggbarstats()`) in the YouAnalyser style, with options to hide the violin plot, the data points or the boxplot.
  * `eda_plot()` plots several variables in one plot, labelled with their variable labels: horizontal or vertical bar charts of the means with or without error bars (CI, SE, SD), boxplots, or stacked bar charts of the distributions. Significant differences (tests of `eda_test()`) can be marked: triangles for groups higher / lower than the reference (default: the total) or letters of the groups that a group significantly exceeds.
* `modelbased`, `effectsize`, `marginaleffects` and `sandwich` are now imported (tests of group differences). `shiny`, `bslib`, `DT` (for `eda_explore()`) and `ggstatsplot` (for `eda_plot_detail()`) are suggested.
* Added `sm_synthetic`, a synthetic dataset (based on the 2024 Swissmilk "Milch & Umwelt" study) with the 20 nutrition attitude items used for the segmentation, the original segment membership (`cluster_benchmark`), profiling variables and sociodemographics, for use in upcoming segmentation (cluster analysis) functions.

# YouAnalyser 0.9.2

* Added a Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM) module, wrapping the `pricesensitivitymeter` package and adding YouGov-specific reporting helpers:
  * Re-exported `psm_analysis()` and `psm_analysis_weighted()` from `pricesensitivitymeter` for the (unweighted and weighted) van Westendorp analysis, including the Newton-Miller-Smith (NMS) extension.
  * `psm_design_for_weighted()` builds an unclustered `survey.design` object from a weight column, for use with `psm_analysis_weighted()`.
  * `psm_plot()` creates the classic van Westendorp price sensitivity plot (with the four cumulative distribution curves, indifference and optimal price points, and acceptable price range), plus reach and revenue optimization plots when the NMS extension was used.
  * `psm_save_data_for_chart()` and `psm_copy_pptx_template()` export PSM results into standardized Excel and PowerPoint templates, mirroring the existing KDA reporting workflow.
* Added a new vignette, `vignette("psm")`, demonstrating the PSM workflow end to end.
* Added `chm_synthetic`, a synthetic dataset (based on the 2025 CH Media Oneplus Streaming Markenstudie) with the four basic van Westendorp questions and the two NMS purchase-intention questions, for use in examples and the PSM vignette.

# YouAnalyser 0.9.1

* Initial submission.
* First version of the package, introducing:
  * Data processing (`dp_*`) functions for preparing and cleaning survey data (e.g., labelling, recoding missing values).
  * Exploratory data analysis (`eda_*`) functions for summarizing and visualizing survey data.
